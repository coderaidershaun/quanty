They are the same concept when both names mean one and the same idea: another spelling, an abbreviation, a longer or a shorter name, or a symbol and its spelled-out name. Examples of the same concept:
- "Black–Scholes model" and "Black–Scholes option pricing model"
- "risk-free rate" and "risk-free interest rate"
- "strike" and "strike price"
- "d₁" and "d one"

They are different concepts, even when they are closely related, in these cases:
- A model, the equation it leads to and the formula that solves it. "Black–Scholes model", "Black–Scholes equation" and "Black–Scholes formula" are three concepts.
- A general idea and one kind of it. "volatility" and "implied volatility" are different, and so are "option" and "call option", and "European option" and "European call".
- A thing and its counterpart. "call option" and "put option" are different, and so are "European call" and "European put".

Judge by the definitions as well as by the names. When in doubt, answer that they are different.

Return `same`: true when the two are the same concept, false when they are different.
