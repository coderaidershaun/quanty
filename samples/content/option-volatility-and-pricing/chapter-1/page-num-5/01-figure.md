Figure 13-4 is a line chart of theoretical profit or loss against underlying price for three option spreads.

The vertical axis, "Theoretical profit or loss", runs from -50 to +10 in steps of 5 (labelled +10, +5, 0, -5, -10, -15, -20, -25, -30, -35, -40, -45, -50). The horizontal axis, "Underlying price", runs from 38 to 58 in steps of 2.

"Spread 1 (short straddle)" is a solid line forming an inverted V: it peaks at about +6 near 48.4 and falls steeply on both sides, reaching -50 near 44 on the left and below -50 near 54 on the right. "Spread 2 (ratio spread)" is a dashed line that is flat at about 0 to +3 on the left, rises to the peak near 48.4, then falls steeply to the right. "Spread 3 (long butterfly)" is a dotted line, a bell-shaped curve that is about -40 at 38, rises to a peak near 48.4, then falls to about -40 at 58.

A circle at the peak marks the call-out: "At an underlying price of 48.40, all three spreads show approximately the same theoretical profit." An arrow points up at the peak.

The figure shows that the three spreads have about the same profit near 48.40 but very different risk when the underlying moves away: the straddle has unlimited loss on both sides, the ratio spread is protected on the downside, and the butterfly has limited loss in both directions.
