Before expiration an option is usually worth more than the payoff it would give today. That payoff is its *intrinsic value*: for a call, the stock price minus the strike when that is positive, and zero otherwise. Whatever the market pays above that is its *time value*, the price of the chance that the stock moves further in the holder's favour before the right runs out. Five things set the price: the price of the underlying, the strike, the time left to expiration, the volatility of the underlying and the interest rate.
