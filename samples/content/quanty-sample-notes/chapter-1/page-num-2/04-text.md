Table 1-1 is read one row at a time. A higher stock price helps a call and hurts a put, and a higher strike does the reverse. More volatility always helps the holder of either kind, and more time does so as a rule, because both widen the range of places the stock can end up, and the floor of zero lets the holder keep the good outcomes without suffering the bad ones. The interest rate usually matters least. It favours the call because the buyer of a call pays the strike later, and paying later is worth more when money earns more. The holder of a put is paid the strike later, so the same rise works against the put.
