The *Black–Scholes model* rests on a simple observation. Hold one call option and sell short the right amount of the underlying stock, and for a very short moment the combined position does not care whether the stock ticks up or down: the gain on one side cancels the loss on the other.[^2] A position with no risk must earn the risk-free interest rate, no more and no less, or someone could collect a free profit. Setting the return of the position equal to the risk-free rate is what pins down a price for the option.
