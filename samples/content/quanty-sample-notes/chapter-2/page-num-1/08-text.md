The first term is the *drift*: on average the stock grows at the rate \( \mu \). The second term is the random part. \( W \) is a *Brownian motion*, a random path whose change over \( dt \) has mean zero and variance \( dt \), and \( \sigma \) is the *volatility*, which scales that randomness. Because both terms are proportional to \( S \), a stock worth ten times as much moves ten times as far in dollars, and its price can never become negative.
