In this equation the expected growth rate \( \mu \) of the stock has disappeared, which is the result that the first chapter described in words: the price of the option does not depend on how fast the stock is expected to rise. The equation alone has many solutions, though. The one that describes an option is picked out by its value on the expiration date, when \( V \) must equal \( \max(S - K, 0) \) for a call and \( \max(K - S, 0) \) for a put. Read as a balance, the first two terms are what the hedged position gains in a short time, from the passing of time and from the curvature of the option as the stock moves, and the last two terms take away the interest that the position must earn, so nothing is left over. The curvature term is where volatility enters: the more the stock moves, the more the curvature is worth.
